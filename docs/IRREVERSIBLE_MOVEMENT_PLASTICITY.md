# Irreversible Plasticity of Movement: Theoretical Framework & Mechanism Design

> Grounding the Movement simulation in **Botero et al. (2014)**, **Umehara et al. (2007)**, and **Pfennig (2021)**.

---

## 1. Research Motivation & Core Premise

The overarching goal of the **Movement** project is to experimentally test the evolutionary predictions of **Botero et al. (2014)** (*Evolutionary tipping points in the capacity to adapt to environmental change*, *PNAS*):

> **Core Theorem**: The combination of **environmental predictability ($P$)** and the **relative timescale of variation ($R$)** partitions the evolutionary parameter space into discrete, non-overlapping regimes of adaptive strategies:
> 1. **Reversible Phenotypic Plasticity** (fast fluctuations, high predictability)
> 2. **Irreversible / Developmental Plasticity** (intermediate timescales, moderate/high predictability)
> 3. **Bet-Hedging** (conservative CBH or diversified DBH; low predictability)
> 4. **Adaptive Tracking** (slow generational drift; $\log R > 3$)
> 
> Crossing the boundaries between these regimes results in catastrophic evolutionary tipping points and population collapse.

In **Movement**, rather than modeling abstract phenotypic traits (like insulation), the evolved phenotype is **locomotion and spatial foraging behavior**.

---

## 2. The Ecological Dilemma: Swimming vs. Settling

Movement is not an energetically free action; it requires the synthesis and maintenance of an active motor apparatus (e.g., bacterial flagella, cilia, or muscular tissue).

- **The Energetic Burden of Motility**:
  - In biological systems, the flagellar motor apparatus imposes continuous basal metabolic maintenance costs even when idling.
- **The Finding of Umehara et al. (2007)**:
  - When *E. coli* cells experience prolonged starvation, they undergo **deflagellation** (shedding flagellar filaments) to shed maintenance costs and enter a low-energy quiescent survival state.
  - When food is restored, growth resumes immediately, but cells **remain completely immotile for up to 6 generations** due to the massive regulatory and energetic barrier to flagellogenesis.
- **The Evolutionary Trade-off**:
  - **Motile Disperser**: Can flee depleting patches and navigate spatial gradients, but pays continuous metabolic overhead (`flagella_maintenance_cost` + movement energy).
  - **Sessile Grazer**: Permanently immotile, but saves maintenance energy and excels at surviving local downturns if the patch remains viable.

## 3. Sensory Cue Specification

In spatial ecology, abiotic resources alone do not dictate fitness; local competitor density determines actual intake.

We define the primary external cue $C(x, t)$ perceived by an agent at position $x$ and time $t$ as **per-capita local resource availability**:

$$C(x, t) = \frac{E(x, t)}{1 + N_{\text{competitors}}(x, t)}$$

Where:
- $E(x, t) \in [0, 1]$: Raw environmental resource level generated by the time-varying Perlin noise field.
- $N_{\text{competitors}}(x, t)$: Number of competing individuals occupying or adjacent to the same hex cell.

### Why Per-Capita Resource is the Canonical Cue:
1. **Unifies Abiotic and Biotic Predictability**:
   - $E(x, t)$ captures physical environmental variation (spatial smoothness $P_{\text{space}}$ and temporal drift $P_{\text{time}}$).
   - $N_{\text{competitors}}$ captures density-dependent competition and game-theoretic crowding.
2. **Biophysically Realistic**:
   - Single-cell organisms and grazers sense nutrient flux through receptor occupancy, which directly reflects nutrient availability diluted by neighbor uptake.

## 4. Strictly Irreversible Mechanism (Phase 1 Specification)

For the initial implementation, we implement a **strictly irreversible within-lifetime mechanism**:

```
                       Birth / Juvenile Stage (t = 0)
                                     |
                         [Evaluate Cue C(x, 0)]
                                     |
               +---------------------+---------------------+
               |                                           |
         C(x, 0) >= θ_settle                         C(x, 0) < θ_settle
               |                                           |
       [Motile Disperser]                           [Sessile Grazer]
       - Flagella Active (F = 1)                    - Flagella Shed (F = 0)
       - Movement Enabled (Hex Steps)               - Movement Permanently Disabled
       - Pays: C_base + C_flagella                  - Pays: C_base only (Discounted)
               |                                           |
    [Starvation Deflagellation]                            |
    - If Energy < E_starve: F -> 0                         |
    - IRREVERSIBLE: Locked immotile                        |
               \                                           /
                +-----------------+-----------------------+
                                  |
                           Adult Lifetime
```

### Mathematical Formulation:
1. **Motility State $F \in \{0, 1\}$**:
   - Each individual carries a discrete flagellar state $F$.
   - At birth, $F$ is determined either genetically or developmentally via a sensory reaction norm:
     $$F_0 = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } f_{\text{brain}}(C_{\text{birth}}) > 0 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
2. **Permanent In-Lifetime Deflagellation Trigger**:
   - If an active disperser ($F=1$) encounters prolonged starvation:
     $$\text{Energy}_t < E_{\text{starve}} \implies F \to 0$$
   - Once $F = 0$, the state is **strictly locked** for the remainder of that individual's life. No subsequent sensory input or energy surge can restore motility within that lifetime.
3. **Metabolic Accounting**:
   - Active Disperser ($F=1$):
     $$\text{Drain}_t = C_{\text{basal}} + C_{\text{flagella}} + \text{Cost}(\text{MovementStep})$$
   - Sessile Grazer ($F=0$):
     $$\text{Drain}_t = C_{\text{basal}} \quad (\text{Movement disabled, } \text{Cost}(\text{Movement}) = 0)$$

## 5. Evolutionary Predictions on the Botero Landscape

By conducting parameter sweeps across **Timescale of Environmental Variation** ($R$, controlled via Perlin time-scale) and **Predictability** ($P$, controlled via spatial gradient contrast and noise):

1. **High Predictability, Rapid Fluctuations ($P \to 1, R < 1$)**:
   - Reversible active taxis is favored; organisms maintain $F = 1$ and follow resource waves.
2. **Intermediate Timescale, Patchy Heterogeneity ($R \sim 1$–$5$, $P \approx 0.5$)**:
   - **Irreversible developmental plasticity emerges**:
     - Offspring born in rich, uncrowded patches ($C \gg 1$) shed flagella immediately, saving metabolic tax and grazing the patch.
     - Offspring born in barren or overcrowded patches ($C \ll 1$) retain $F=1$ to disperse into new regions.
3. **Zero Predictability ($P \to 0$)**:
   - Cues are deceptive. Populations evolve bet-hedging:
     - **Conservative (CBH)**: Complete loss of motility across the entire population (permanent sessile generalists).
     - **Diversified (DBH)**: Genotypes produce a stochastic 50/50 ratio of motile dispersers and sessile grazers regardless of local cue.

---

## 6. Future Extension: Continuous Evolvable Reversibility

In future iterations, the reversibility parameter $a \in [0, 1]$ (from Botero et al.) will be modeled as a continuous property:
- **Flagellar Regeneration Cost & Delay ($T_{\text{grow}}$)**:
  - Re-growing flagella requires an energetic investment sustained over $T_{\text{grow}}$ ticks.
  - As $T_{\text{grow}} \to 0$, plasticity becomes fully **reversible**.
  - As $T_{\text{grow}} \to \text{Lifespan}$, plasticity becomes **irreversible**.
  - Evolution can optimize the recovery delay $T_{\text{grow}}$ as a function of environmental cycle length.
